▦ Bitcrusher (bitcrusher)

What it's for

A lo-fi digital distortion effect that reduces bit depth/resolution, giving a gritty, crunchy, "broken digital" or retro-game character. Great on drums, basses, or anything you want to sound deliberately dirty.

Inputs

  • In — the audio signal to crush.
  • Bypass — turns the effect off without unpatching it.
  • Crush — how aggressive the effect is; higher usually means grittier/more distorted.

Output

Out — the crushed audio signal.

〰 Wave Folder (wave-folder)

What it's for

A wave-folding distortion, in the style of West Coast synth modules (think Buchla/Serge). Instead of clipping the peaks like normal distortion, it "folds" the waveform back on itself once it passes a threshold, producing rich, harmonically complex, often bell-like or metallic timbres out of a simple wave.

Inputs

  • In — the audio signal to fold.
  • Drive — pushes the signal harder into folding for more harmonics.
  • Symmetry — shifts the folding off-center for asymmetrical, more complex tones.
  • Gain — output level trim.
  • Mix — blends the folded signal with the original dry signal.

Output

Out — the folded audio signal.

◟◝ Envelope Follower (envelope-follower)

What it's for

Tracks the volume/loudness of an audio signal over time and turns it into a CV — roughly the opposite direction of an ADSR (which shapes a sound): this instead reads a sound's natural dynamics and turns them into a controllable modulation source. Classic use: follow a drum loop or vocal and use the result to drive a filter for an auto-wah, or to duck another sound.

Inputs

  • In — the audio signal to follow.
  • Attack — how quickly it reacts when the signal gets louder.
  • Release — how quickly it falls back down when the signal gets quieter.
  • PreGain — boosts the input before tracking, for quiet sources.

Output

Out — the tracked loudness as CV, patch it into a filter cutoff, gain, or anywhere else you want it to react to the input's dynamics.

⇄ Pan (pan)

What it's for

Positions a mono signal somewhere between two outputs, left and right — a stereo panner. Patch a CV into its Pan input for auto-panning effects, or set it by hand to place a sound in the stereo field.

Inputs

  • In — the signal to pan.
  • Pan — the pan position (patch an LFO here for automatic left-right movement).

Outputs

Out1 / Out2 — the panned left/right signals, patch into a stereo Mixer or output widget.

🌀 Chorus (chorus)

What it's for

A classic chorus effect — mixes the signal with slightly delayed, pitch-wobbled copies of itself to sound thicker and wider, like several instruments playing in unison instead of one.

Inputs

  • In — the audio signal to thicken.
  • Bypass — turns the effect off without unpatching it.
  • Rate — how fast the internal modulation wobbles.
  • Depth — how strong the wobble/thickening is.
  • Offset — shifts the internal delay time, changing the character of the effect.

Outputs

Out L / Out R — the widened stereo signal.

🔁 Delay (delay)

What it's for

A stereo echo/delay effect — repeats the incoming signal after a set time, with feedback for multiple repeats. Use it for anything from a slap-back echo to long rhythmic, dubby repeats.

Inputs

  • In L / In R — the stereo signal to delay.
  • Bypass — turns the effect off without unpatching it.
  • Time — how long before each echo repeats.
  • Feedback — how many times it repeats (higher feeds more of the echo back into itself).
  • Mix — blends the delayed signal with the dry original.

Outputs

Out L / Out R — the delayed stereo signal.

🪶 Reverb Light (reverb-light)

What it's for

A lightweight reverb — simulates the natural reflections of a room or space, giving a sound depth and ambience instead of sounding dry and close. "Light" means it's tuned to be efficient enough to run happily alongside everything else in your patch.

Inputs

  • In L / In R — the stereo signal to add reverb to.
  • Bypass — turns the effect off without unpatching it.
  • Room Size — how large the simulated space feels.
  • Damping — how quickly high frequencies die out in the reflections, for a brighter or darker space.
  • Wet Level — how much of the reverb signal is added.
  • Dry Level — how much of the original, un-reverbed signal remains.

Outputs

Out L / Out R — the reverberated stereo signal.
