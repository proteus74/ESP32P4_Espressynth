Generator

Die Klangerzeuger von ESPRESSYNTH: Oszillatoren, eine Hammond-Orgel-Emulation, Sample-Player, ein SoundFont-Player und ein klassisches FM-Piano. Jeder Generator liefert ein Audiosignal, das anschließend gefiltert, moduliert und gemischt werden kann.

🔔 Oscillator (fm-oscillator)

What it's for

This is ESPRESSYNTH's main sound source — a voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO), just like the core oscillator in a subtractive or FM synth voice. It generates a tone at a pitch you control, and can also be frequency-modulated by another oscillator for FM/bell-like and metallic sounds.

Key inputs

  • Pitch — the 1V/oct-style pitch CV, normally patched from Midi-In's Pitch output.
  • Tune — fine/coarse tuning offset, for detuning against other oscillators.
  • Amplitude — overall loudness of the oscillator, often patched from Velocity or an envelope.
  • Waveform — picks the wave shape (sine, saw, square, etc.).
  • Gate — retriggers the oscillator's phase/feedback state when a new note starts.
  • Gain — output level trim.
  • Feedback (Self) — feeds the oscillator's own output back into itself for extra harmonic edge/distortion.
  • FM In — patch another oscillator's output here to frequency-modulate this one (classic FM synthesis).
  • FM Ratio — sets the modulator-to-carrier frequency ratio for FM.
  • Duty Cycle — pulse width for the square wave (thin, nasal square vs. fat 50% square).
  • SuperSaw Detune / SuperSaw Mix — stacks and detunes multiple saws under the hood for a thick "supersaw" trance-style sound, and blends how much of that stacking is mixed in.

Output

Out — the resulting audio signal. Patch it into a Filter, Gain/VCA, Mixer, or straight to an output widget.

🎹 Hammond (hammond)

What it's for

A tonewheel organ voice, modeled after the classic Hammond drawbar organ. Instead of one waveform, nine "drawbars" each add a different harmonic (like the nine drawbars on a real Hammond), so you can dial in your own organ registration by ear.

Key inputs

  • Pitch / Tune — the note to play and a tuning offset.
  • Amplitude — overall loudness, typically patched from Velocity.
  • Gain — output level trim.
  • 16′, 5⅓′, 8′, 4′, 2⅔′, 2′, 1⅗′, 1⅓′, 1′ — the nine drawbars, named by the pipe-organ foot-length convention. Each is its own CV input, so you can patch an LFO or envelope to one drawbar to make the tone evolve, or just set them by hand for a classic registration (e.g. 888000000 is a popular starting point).

Unlike most instrument widgets, Hammond has no Gate or Waveform input — the tonewheels spin continuously, and note on/off is shaped entirely by the Amplitude CV and whatever ADSR/Gain you patch downstream.

Output

Out — the organ's audio signal.

🎵 Wave Player (wave-player)

What it's for

Plays back a sample/wave file you've loaded, pitched to the note you play — like a simple sampler. Good for one-shots, loops, or using a recorded instrument instead of a synthesized waveform.

Inputs

  • Amplitude — loudness, typically from Velocity or an envelope.
  • Note (0–127) — which pitch to play the sample at.
  • Gate — starts/stops (or retriggers) playback.
  • Gain — output level trim.

Outputs

Out L / Out R — the stereo audio signal, patch both into a stereo effect or output widget.

🎼 SoundFont Player (soundfont-player)

What it's for

Plays notes using a SoundFont (.sf2) instrument bank — think orchestral instruments, GM instrument sets, or any sample-based patch someone has packaged as a SoundFont. It's a full multi-sample instrument rather than a single oscillator.

Inputs

  • MIDI — the note stream to play, patched from Midi-In's MIDI output (or Midi-Split).
  • Bank / Program — selects which instrument inside the SoundFont to use (GM bank/program numbers).
  • Enable CC Messages / Enable Mod Wheel / Enable Pitch Wheel — lets you turn on/off whether incoming MIDI controller data, mod wheel, and pitch wheel affect the sound.
  • Gain — output level trim.

Outputs

Out L / Out R — the stereo audio signal.

🎹 MDA Piano (mda-piano)

What it's for

A physically-modeled acoustic piano voice (based on the classic mda ePiano), with its own drive, tremolo/wah, and chorus/phaser built in — a ready-to-play electric/acoustic piano sound rather than something you build from scratch.

Inputs

  • MIDI — the note stream to play.
  • Enable Mod Wheel / Enable Pitch Wheel — lets your MIDI controller's wheels affect the sound.
  • Voice Type — picks a piano character/model variant.
  • Drive — adds harmonic saturation/grit.
  • Trem/Wah Mode, Depth, Rate — configures the built-in tremolo or auto-wah effect.
  • Cho/Pha Mode, Depth, Speed — configures the built-in chorus or phaser effect.
  • Pre Gain / Gain — input and output level trims.

Outputs

Out L / Out R — the stereo piano signal.
