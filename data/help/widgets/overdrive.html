What it's for

Classic overdrive and distortion. The signal is boosted by Drive and pushed through a saturation curve that rounds off or clips the peaks, adding harmonics from warm tube-like grit to square-wave fuzz. The firmware runs the curve with 2x oversampling to reduce aliasing.

Inputs

  • In — the audio signal to distort.
  • Mode — character in four steps: Soft (Tube-Screamer style overdrive: only the mids and highs are clipped, the clean bass stays), Hard (full-band hard clipping), Tube (asymmetric, warm even harmonics), Fuzz (pulse-like, octave-heavy fuzz).
  • Drive — amount of distortion, 0 dB to +40 dB of gain into the clipper. Auto-level keeps the distorted signal as loud as the input, so more drive means more grit, not more volume.
  • Tone — low-pass after the distortion (500 Hz – 12 kHz) to tame harsh fizz.
  • Bias — shifts the signal off-center before the curve (relative to the input level) for asymmetric, more complex tones - audible at any drive. The resulting DC offset is removed again.
  • Mix — blends the distorted signal with the original dry signal.
  • Gain — overall output volume.

Every control is a 0–10 V CV input and can be modulated or driven by a Knob.

Output

Out — the distorted audio signal.
