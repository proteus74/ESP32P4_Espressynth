What it's for

Midi-In is the door where a MIDI keyboard, DAW, or other MIDI gear enters your patch. It listens to incoming MIDI notes and converts them into CV signals that the rest of ESPRESSYNTH's modular-style widgets understand — a pitch voltage for an oscillator, a gate for an envelope, and so on.

Inputs

  • Voices — how many notes can sound at once (polyphony). Patch a CV or set it directly to control voice stealing.
  • Glide — turns portamento (pitch sliding between notes) on or off.
  • Glide Time — how long the pitch takes to slide from one note to the next.

Outputs

  • Pitch — the played note as a pitch CV. Patch this into an oscillator's Pitch input to play it.
  • Gate — goes high while a key is held and low when released. Feed this into an ADSR or envelope to shape each note.
  • Velocity — how hard the key was struck, as CV. Patch into an amplifier or filter for expressive playing.
  • Pitch Bend — your pitch-bend wheel position, as bipolar CV centered at 0 V. In Settings, enter the voltage for one side (0–5 V): 1 V gives −1 to +1 V, 3 V gives −3 to +3 V. Patch directly into an oscillator's Tune input to bend all voices; 1 V equals one octave. Existing presets retain their bend range.
  • Aftertouch — how hard you're pressing a held key, as CV.
  • MIDI — the raw MIDI stream, for feeding into other MIDI-aware widgets like Midi-Split.
  • MIDI-Note — the raw MIDI note number (0–127), handy for driving a Value Mapper or sequencer logic.
  • MIDI-ProgChange — the last Program Change number (0–127) received on this Midi-In's channel. After loading a preset it starts at the value saved in the preset. Patch it into a Value Mapper, Input-Switch or Multiplexer to switch sounds from your keyboard's program buttons.
