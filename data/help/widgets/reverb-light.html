What it's for

A stereo plate reverb — simulates the dense reflections of a room or hall, giving a sound depth and ambience instead of sounding dry and close. The tail is dense and smooth: high frequencies die away faster than lows (like in a real room), and the reflections are gently modulated so no metallic ringing builds up. Left and right outputs carry different reflections for a wide stereo image.

Inputs

  • In L / In R — the stereo signal to add reverb to.
  • Bypass — turns the effect off without unpatching it.
  • Room Size — how large the simulated space feels (reverb time about 0.1 to 8 seconds).
  • Damping — how quickly high frequencies die out in the reflections, for a brighter or darker space.
  • Wet Level — how much of the reverb signal is added.
  • Dry Level — how much of the original, un-reverbed signal remains.

Outputs

Out L / Out R — the reverberated stereo signal.
