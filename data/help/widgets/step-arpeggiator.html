What it's for

A monophonic step sequencer/arpeggiator — sixteen knobs, each defining a pitch step, played back one after another as a clock pulses it forward. Great for classic arpeggio and sequenced-bassline patterns without needing a DAW.

Inputs

  • Trigger — advances to the next step on each pulse (patch from a Clock widget, or leave unpatched to use BPM timing).
  • BPM — accepts tempo directly (120 means 120 BPM), for example from Clock's BPM output. Without a cable, Master Clock supplies tempo. Generates four steps per beat while Trigger is unpatched; a connected Trigger controls individual steps.
  • Gate — gates the sequencer on/off, e.g. from a held key.
  • Pitch — an optional pitch CV added on top of the steps, useful for transposing the whole pattern by playing a key.
  • Direction — picks Forward, Backward, Ping-Pong, or Random playback order.
  • Gate-Out-Time — how long each step's gate output stays high.
  • Step 1–16 — the pitch value for each of the sixteen steps.

Skip on C0

Enable Skip on C0 in the widget settings to turn steps at 0 V into rests. Each rest consumes one trigger, keeps the previous CV and sends no Gate Out pulse. It also ends any Gate Out pulse still running from the previous step. The zero-value knob is dimmed but remains editable. Connected step CVs are evaluated before applying Pitch. The option is saved with the preset and defaults to off.

Snap to Semitones

Enable Snap to Semitones in settings to quantize all step values, including mouse movement, MIDI-controlled knobs and external step CVs. Without Pitch connected, the spacing is 1/12 V. With Pitch connected, spacing follows Max Pitch Offset. The ESP engine applies the same quantization during standalone playback, without a browser. The option is saved with the preset and defaults to off.

Outputs

  • CV — the current step's pitch, patch into an oscillator's Pitch.
  • Gate Out — a gate pulse for each step, patch into an ADSR's Gate to re-shape each note.
