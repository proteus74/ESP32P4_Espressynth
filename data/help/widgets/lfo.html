What it's for

A Low Frequency Oscillator — a slow, repeating wave used to wobble other things over time rather than to make an audible tone itself. Patch it into a filter's cutoff for a wah-wah sweep, into pitch for vibrato, into amplitude for tremolo, or anywhere else you want automatic, cyclic movement.

Inputs

  • Rate — how fast it cycles.
  • Amplitude — how big the wobble is.
  • Phase — where in its cycle it starts.
  • Reset — a trigger input that restarts the cycle from the beginning, handy for syncing the wobble to a new note.
  • One Shot — makes the LFO run through its shape once instead of looping.
  • Global — lets several LFO widgets share one free-running clock instead of each running independently.
  • Offset — shifts the wave up or down so it isn't centered on zero.
  • Morph — only in Wavetable mode: picks the position in the wavetable (0 V = first slot, 10 V = last slot, in between the neighbouring slots are crossfaded). Patch another LFO or an envelope in to sweep through the table automatically.

Wavetable mode

The last waveform button (WT) switches the LFO from its fixed shapes to a wavetable: a row of up to 16 self-drawn single-cycle waveforms that the Morph control blends between. Rate, Amplitude, Phase, Offset, Reset, One Shot and Global keep working exactly as with the other shapes. The small preview shows the waveform at the current morph position, with a line marking where in the cycle the LFO currently is; click it or Edit to open the editor:

  • Slots — the bar at the top holds the waveforms in morph order. + adds a copy of the selected slot, − removes it.
  • Presets — Sine, Saw, Square and Triangle fill the selected slot as a starting point.
  • Freehand — draw the waveform directly with the mouse, pen or finger.
  • Harmonics — build the slot additively: each bar is the level of one harmonic (above the line positive, below inverted).
  • Snap to Zero — cuts the waveform at the zero crossings nearest to its start and end and stretches it back to full length, so the loop point doesn't click. While switched on it is re-applied after every edit.

Everything you draw is audible right away - in the browser preview and on the device - even before you press Apply. Apply saves the preset so the new table is kept; Cancel brings the previous table back.

Output

Out — the wobbling CV signal. Patch it wherever you want that slow movement.
